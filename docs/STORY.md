# Badeland: Sea Monster and Story Direction

**Status:** the creative direction for the whole game, from the project owner's brief. Other documents (DESIGN.md, CHAPTER2.md, VERTICAL_SLICE.md) follow this one where they disagree.

The sea monster and the mystery around it are one of the central parts of the game's identity.

## The sea monster

Not a generic Kraken. An **original giant sea creature** that combines a whale, a sea serpent or eel, an octopus, a giant aquatic animal and a fantasy creature. It is enormous compared with the players.

- A large, expressive head
- A gigantic mouth
- Several tentacle-like limbs
- A long body or tail
- Bioluminescent details
- Strange glowing organs or markings
- A stylised cartoon look

It can look slightly cute or funny when calm, and become genuinely intimidating when it attacks. The game should be a little horror and scary.

## The big secret: the monster is a living world

The monster is not simply an animal. It is an ancient creature that has existed for an incredibly long time. Over millions of years a strange ecosystem developed inside its body: first tiny organisms, then plants, then animals, then entire ecosystems. Inside it there are forests, mountains, rivers, lakes, caves, floating islands, strange creatures, ancient ruins, villages, lost settlements.

The players do not understand this at first. They think they are trapped inside a giant creature. Eventually they realise: **there is an entire world inside it.**

## Why does the monster eat people?

Not because it is evil. It consumes things because it needs them to survive or repair itself. A strange biological process turns what it swallows into part of its internal ecosystem. Through history it has accidentally swallowed ships, animals, treasure, buildings, objects, people and parts of the ocean floor, and many of these became part of the world inside.

This gives a discovery mechanic: the players find something inside that clearly came from the outside world. A modern-looking object. A piece of a ship. A waterpark sign. A toy. A piece of an inflatable. They realise other people have been here before.

## The lost people

NPCs live inside the creature. They have been trapped for years, decades, even centuries. Some have made small communities. Some have forgotten the outside world. Others are trying to escape.

One possible reveal: an NPC says they were swallowed many years ago. That raises questions. How many people has it swallowed? How long has it existed? Is there a way out? Has anyone ever escaped? What is it, really?

**Do not explain everything at once.** Let the player discover it through NPC dialogue, environmental storytelling, old notes, ruins, paintings, lost objects, strange symbols, hidden rooms, ancient structures and previous explorers' equipment.

### Decided in the first Chapter 2 area (fixed canon so far)

- **The people who gave themselves.** Many, many years ago a group (the **Tide-Keepers**) believed the creature, which they called **the Bearer**, came from a *sacred place* where the sea was born. They went to it **willingly**, singing, to be taken in, to become part of the world it carries. They left folded robes and offering bowls (forty-one of them) at a shrine inside. It is a gentle, strange sacrifice, not a massacre.
- **The Bearer carries a world so that it does not end.** It cannot carry what it does not have, so the world must be mended with new things. (This matches "it consumes things to repair itself".)
- **Not everyone came by choice.** The people the sea sends now are accidents. The Keepers' last carving apologises to them.
- **Marit and the narrator.** Two travellers who came in through the water. **Marit** goes insane inside: she stops sleeping, shouts at the walls, feels the creature "thinking, and afraid", and one day wades into the lake and swims off toward the far door. The **narrator** becomes lonely, talks to the mushrooms, sees Marit everywhere (the faint figures that vanish when you get close), and believes they can walk out through the great door. They cannot. The last page is blank.

## The waterpark may not be a coincidence

The waterpark may have been built directly above or near where the creature sleeps. The original builders may have known. Modern employees and visitors do not. It may be built around an ancient structure or mechanism that keeps the creature asleep.

**Not explained at the beginning.** The player only finds hints: strange symbols under the waterpark, old locked rooms, ancient machinery, a hidden tunnel, unusual pipes, old maps, a mysterious room under the main pool, objects much older than the waterpark. At first they look like secrets or easter eggs. Later the player realises they are connected to the monster.

## The treasures may be connected too

The hidden treasures in the waterpark should not all be random collectibles. Some belong to the creature or to its history. The players unknowingly collect objects the creature recognises. When they reach the floating inflatable platform at the end, the monster is not attacking randomly. **It has noticed them.** It may be following the objects they collected. They have accidentally awakened or attracted it.

## The final waterpark sequence

After the waterpark section all players reach a large floating inflatable platform. At first: bright sunlight, happy music, calm water, colour, celebration. Then the atmosphere changes: the music stops, the water goes unnaturally still, the lights fade, something huge moves beneath. The players look down. Nothing. Then the monster attacks.

The fight is ultimately **unwinnable**. The players can dodge, jump, help each other, use objects and attack weak points, but the creature destroys the platform and eats them. Cut to black.

## Inside the monster

The players wake up somewhere unexpected, in a cut scene. They are alive. At first it seems like a strange cave. Then water. Then plants. Then animals. Then a massive landscape. Eventually they realise this is **not a stomach. It is an entire world.** This begins the second major part of Badeland.

## The monster may not be the villain

As they explore the players discover the creature may be suffering. It may be sick. Its internal ecosystem may be collapsing. It consumes things to survive or repair itself. The player eventually decides what to do.

- **Ending 1: kill the creature.** The players escape, but the world inside may die too.
- **Ending 2: save the creature.** They travel deep, find what makes it sick, repair or remove it. The creature survives, and the players may find another way out.
- **Ending 3: the secret ending.** If the players found enough hidden treasures and secrets, they learn the real reason the creature exists: perhaps it protects something much older and more dangerous at the bottom of the ocean. This changes how the player understands the whole story.

## Story principle

Do not reveal everything at once. Keep the player asking: *"This is a fun waterpark game." Then: "Why are there all these strange secrets?" Then: "OMG there is a giant sea monster?" Then: "Why are we still alive?" Then: "WHAT IS THIS WORLD INSIDE THE MONSTER?" And finally: "Wait... the monster might not actually be the bad guy."*

## Tone

Colourful adventure, comedy, fast action, exploration, mystery, fantasy, occasional tension, character moments. Never relentlessly dark. The contrast is the defining feature: laughing as you fall off an inflatable course, then standing silent in a dark ocean while something enormous moves underneath you.

## The core progression

Badeland, fun waterpark, hidden traps and treasures, something strange is happening, the sea monster appears, the players are eaten, they discover a world inside the monster, they discover others were trapped there, they uncover the history of the creature, they discover the waterpark is connected to it, they discover the monster may not be evil, they decide the creature's fate.

The goal: the player finishes the first major section thinking *"I thought I was playing a waterpark game. What the hell is this?"*

## Next steps for the story (proposal)

1. Place the first hints in the waterpark (a symbol under the main pool, a locked room, odd old pipes). Reuse the hidden-trap system for "secret rooms".
2. Make the treasures count: save the number across scenes, and let the creature react to it (the eyes, a line of text, a different first note inside).
3. Give the monster a more original body (whale-like body, long tail, expressive face) and a calm "sleeping" moment under the waterpark.
4. Build area 2 of Chapter 2 and the first lost NPC community (see CHAPTER2.md).
5. Write the middle of the story so that "the monster may be sick" becomes visible in the world: parts of the interior that are dying, and a first sign of *what* is making it sick.
